Hello,

Regarding…

> it'll be interesting to see if like... there's a new "pricing model" for the internet for humans and bots to surf/work/buy separately. If you have thoughts on this, I'd love to hear them!

…perhaps orienting all of society around a single value (profit) tends toward warped and perverse outcomes in all things? If all efforts are truly aimed at profit, then all other benefits are inherently secondary, _subservient_.

Accessibility, as you mentioned, while ranking highly in a moralist’s view, is only a small slice from a long tail of diminishing returns for profit. So of course it’s coming more into focus as LLMs provide the leverage (and necessity?) to squeeze every last drop from the fruits of labor. 

So would we have one internet _for_ people and one _for_ bots? Or is it more likely we have a wasteland internet that isn’t _for_ anyone and a digital Elysium in the cloud for and of a wealthy few? That seems to be the plan for the physical world, after all. 

This world isn’t _for_ humans, bots, or anyone — _it’s for profit_. 
  
From one of your links ([Era of Bleh](https://www.noemamag.com/now-entering-the-era-of-bleh)):

> It’s a funny predicament: all day, humans across the same industry — ostensibly hired for their unique critical thinking and experience — collectively pump every thought, business challenge, idea and solution through the same set of AI tools. Almost as if all these companies have simultaneously hired the same single employee: Mr. Claude (or one of his contemporaries).

This sort of collapsing of things has been on my mind a lot.

We ought to doubt that the paradigm now pursued by “AI” business at large will ever achieve anything remotely warranting the audacious name they’ve given themselves, let alone “artificial _general_ intelligence”, no matter how much money, water, minerals, and energy they throw at it. They’re trying to brute force their way into creating consciousness but they don’t even know what it is. 

…but for fun, I do imagine if they succeeded…

I don’t think even most of the folks who are fully bought in to the idea really grasp the enormity of what they’re doing. We talk about how it will change software development, but why should we assume there will still be software? Why would we design, code, ship software at all if the computer could generate exactly the necessary interface for any given task on demand? We wouldn’t. We would have to completely reimagine what it means to use a computer. We would need different operating systems, maybe even the physical form of the machine would change. Yet we keep shoving these “AI” tools into the current information framework: turning them around and trying differently shaped holes to see where it fits. 

My point is: we need to reimagine the whole system. We need to figure out what we value as a society, and reorganize ourselves around those principles. We need to stop taking every block, every shape, and [putting it in the square hole](https://youtu.be/6pDH66X3ClA).
