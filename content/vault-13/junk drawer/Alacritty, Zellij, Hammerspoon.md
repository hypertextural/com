Or, my journey to setting up a new programming flow. 

(Catppuccin, Helix)


I wanted to try a new terminal because I was having getting some slow responses with my autocomplete when using Helix in iTerm. Also because I love to get distracted and spend a day or two reworking my setup about once a year. 

I looked around at what terminal emulators people were using these days and identified a few. 

- WezTerm
- Rio
- Alacritty
- Kitty
- Foot (I’m on MacOS and at time of writing I think this one is Linux only, but it’s users seem very happy with it.)
- Warp

Kitty piqued my interest because it’s called Kitty. So that’s what I looked at first. It has a ton of features and I don’t always like that. Sometimes I do, but at least right now I don’t feel like getting involved with it. 

I heard some people saying nice things about WezTerm, but I have little bit of the feature overwhelm here that I got with Kitty, combined with some (potentially meaningless) benchmarks that suggested it wasn’t much faster than iTerm, and that _had (ostensibly)_ been the point of my switch. 

I took a look at Warp, because, you know, it’s very shiny. But the commercial angle really rubs me the wrong way, and I am decidedly _not jazzed_ about giving them my email, letting them phone home, their AI integrations, or imagining what happens to the “product” three years from now when some investor wants to see more growth. Big hard pass on that. 

That left Rio and Alacritty. Rio seems interesting but there’s a lot of talk about how fast Alacritty is (I see now the developers are extremely focused on speed) and Rio seemed to be more of an unknown, so I went with Alacritty.
