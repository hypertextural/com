At TTS, we value diversity, equity, inclusion and accessibility. Can you tell us about something you’ve done to make co-workers or team members feel **included** and **empowered**? Or, can you share what you’ve done in situations when you’ve **observed non-inclusive behaviors**?

### Response

I believe in systemic and institutional racism, sexism, and bias. I think that ourselves and our pasts create our society and that our society also creates us. I think it’s important that we recognize that all societies are flawed and that we necessarily contain those flaws. By reconciling ourselves with this we can accept our mistakes, give ourselves space to be wrong, give others space to be wrong, avoid reaction and aggravated defense, and be able to work towards more inclusive, more perfect communities, that are less divided, less lonely, and more resilient through their diversity. 

RE diversity: the interesting thing about this is that it’s a very pervasive thing that finds its way into our interactions constantly. 

encourage people to take time to learn how to do things, help give them the tools to teach themselves

one of the most important things i think i can do as an individual is recognize that i am not free of bias and accept that i can be wrong, and that being wrong is not a binary situation that automatically turns me into a "bad person". then i can examine the biases and begin to untangle them

Something interesting about this question in the context of a behavioral interview is that I think it generally favors highlighting singular situations of high importance or impact, but I feel like the work of including others and the work of unravelling systemic biases is something that requires *constant* consideration and thoughtfulness in everything we do and so it must come from a particular mindset—one that values empathy, consideration, kindness, a willingness to see the flaws in our societies and systems and a willingness to see those flaws reflected in ourselves.
